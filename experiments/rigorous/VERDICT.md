# What these experiments support

The framework completed 66 training runs: 30 learning-rate pilots and 36 evaluated models. Core source/config/data/checkpoint hashes are preserved; all 36 final checkpoints passed the tested future-perturbation and cached-generation checks, including 8192-prefix decoding. Worst long-prefix cached/full logit discrepancy was 6.2e-6. No exact training matches were found among 7669 nonoverlapping 128-character blocks from the final books. These checks reduce concern about specific implementation mistakes; they do not prove general correctness or novelty.

On unseen Frankenstein and Sherlock Holmes, the primary parameter-matched models trained for 1800 updates scored geometric mean character perplexity 6.047 full attention,5.964 local-plus-global, and 5.592 striped. Adding the whole modal branch helped against local-plus-global in all three seeds, including with identical feed-forward widths. The tested system remains a two-layer width 64 character model, and the modal-branch ablation does not isolate Fourier basis choice from every alternative mixer.

The quality advantage is budget-dependent. Interleaved timing gave a median paired stripe/full training-step ratio 1.355. A baseline trained for the resulting 2439 updates scored 5.701 versus striped 5.592, only about 1.9% worse. Literal equal elapsed-time runs scored 5.192 after roughly 4391 updates, but substantial host-load drift distorted that comparison; retain it as a practical noisy result rather than a clean FLOP comparison. The matched-token training speed is not a win for striped.

The exploratory local-only baseline scored 5.857, with only 8.25 KiB retained cache in its paired diagnostic versus 4120.25 KiB striped at 8192. It has a bounded receptive field and cannot establish distant retrieval ability. It was added after the primary results were viewed and is labeled post hoc; its learning rate still used development data only. This exposes a meaningful quality/resource tradeoff and prevents presenting cache savings as uniquely spectral.

Long-context language quality is a limitation. The original continuation test scored 128 targets after a prefix, so even its 128-prefix condition exceeded the trained position range. That valid continuation test is retained with corrected labeling. The corrective fixed-window diagnostic uses exactly the specified history and one target, seed 0,64 positions/book. On those identical targets, context 128-trained striped goes from 4.599 PPL at 128 history to 8.781 at 8192; context 512-trained striped goes from 5.071 to 6.873. This is a small diagnostic, not a broad benchmark, but it rules out assuming that long-prefix timing automatically accompanies preserved predictive quality.

Striped retains a global-attention block. Its whole-model cache is still O(T), and full-sequence attention work is still O(T²). About half the cache is a constant-factor saving. The local-plus-global control also obtains most of that saving. Paired CPU timing and all raw repetitions are in REPORT.md and the JSONs; they establish no GPU result.

An honest engineering blog can discuss a modest quality improvement after equal training characters, the shrinking advantage under calibrated training cost, and the cache/latency/context-quality tradeoff. A methods preprint claiming a novel superior attention replacement is not supported. Further evidence would require larger token models, longer training and tuning beyond the grid endpoint, optimized GPU comparisons, wider tasks, and ablations that distinguish modal memory from other extra gated mixers.

The full study, supplemental plans, exploratory additions and raw outcomes are in REPORT.md, PROTOCOL.md, CALIBRATION_PLAN.md, LOCAL_CONTROL_PLAN.md and runs/. Use run_all.py to preserve an evidence archive before and after reruns.
